# Brief for Claude Design: FLIM's App Store screenshots

Written 2026-09-10, on the 1.5.2 train. Paste the block below into Claude Design as the opening
prompt. It asks for four or five directions for the screenshot set, each shown as its first
three panels, since those are the ones the App Store shows in search results before anyone taps.

---

I'm designing the App Store screenshot set for FLIM, an invite-only iPhone app that is a
disposable camera with one film look. You point and shoot, the frame comes back with the look
already on it, and if you post it, it goes to a feed of only the people who follow you. Rolls are
shared cameras: a group shoots into one roll, nobody sees anything until it develops, then the
reveal plays for everyone at once. There is no algorithm and no public feed. Every shared photo
carries a small orange segment-display date stamp in the bottom corner, like the date back on a
90s point-and-shoot. The app is dark only.

Today the store shows raw device captures with a caption. I want designed panels instead, the
way games do it: a composed image at the store's size that frames a real screen inside it, with
room for a line of copy. The screens themselves have to be real captures of the app (App Review
checks), so every direction must hold a screen at its true proportions, untouched, and do its
work around it.

FORMAT
- Portrait panels at 1320 by 2868 pixels (the 6.9 inch size; the store scales it down for
  smaller phones). Up to ten panels; the first three show in search results and matter most.
- The phone screen inside a panel is 1320 by 2868 at full size, so any framed screen is a
  scaled copy of that shape. Photos inside the app are 3:4 portrait, never square, never cropped.
- Copy per panel: one line, at most six words, readable at thumbnail size. Optional second
  line, at most ten words, that can disappear at small sizes.

THE FIVE SCREENS WE WILL SUPPLY, in store order, with the copy each carries today:
1. The feed. A friend's photo with the film look, six-emoji reaction row, comment field.
   "A feed that's just your friends."
2. The camera. Live viewfinder in a full-width 3:4 box, shutter below, a pill showing a roll
   developing with its countdown. "Shoot like a disposable."
3. Chapters. A profile with a shelf of monthly covers, one tagged RECAP, above a film-strip grid
   of 3:4 frames. "Every month you shared, kept."
4. The month in numbers. A dark closing card: most reacted photo with its thumbnail, biggest
   fan, golden hour, streak, longest gap. "The month in numbers."
5. Rolls. A developing roll with its countdown at the top, ready rolls under it, the album below.
   "Rolls for your people."
Spares: the reveal mid-play with its film strip; a roll grid with a burst stack showing a count.

WHAT TO DESIGN
Four or five distinct directions for the whole set. For each, show panels 1 to 3 fully composed
with placeholder screens (a dark rectangle at the right proportions is fine, or a blurred
stand-in), plus a short note on how panels 4 to 10 follow the system. Directions I want to see
argued for, not necessarily all of them:
- The screen as a print: the phone screen sits inside a paper-white or film-black photo border
  with the orange date stamp burned into the border, copy set like a caption under a print.
- The film strip: the three panels read as consecutive frames of one strip, sprocket holes and
  frame numbers running across the set, each frame holding a screen.
- Full-bleed photograph: a FLIM photograph fills the panel edge to edge, the screen floats
  small over it, the copy sits in the dark part of the image.
- The camera body: the screen sits where a disposable camera's viewfinder would, the rest of the
  panel is the matte black body, the copy is embossed where the brand would be.
- Bare and typographic: no frame, the screen at nearly full size, one huge line of copy top or
  bottom, nothing else.

CONSTRAINTS
- Colours: black, white, greys, the orange of the date stamp, and nothing else. No gradients
  except the film look's own vignette inside photographs.
- Type: one sans for copy. The orange stamp is a fourteen-segment display; if you set a date in
  it, make it a real fourteen-segment form, not a rounded LCD font.
- No exclamation marks. No em dashes. No "the number one", no stars, no awards.
- No fake UI inside the screens. No drawn phone bezels that misrepresent the device; a bezel is
  fine if it is neutral and does not claim a model.
- No recognizable faces in any placeholder or example image.
- Copy is short, declarative, and plain: "A feed that's just your friends." is the register.

WHAT I NEED BACK
Per direction: three composed 1320 by 2868 panels, one line naming the direction, three lines on
how it extends to the rest of the set, and the copy you would put on each panel if you would
change it. Then one recommendation with the reason in two sentences.
