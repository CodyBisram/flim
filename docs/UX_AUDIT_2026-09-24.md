# FLIM 1.5.4 UX audit, and the case for version 2 (September 24, 2026)

## Context and scope

A second full audit of the iOS app, this time as a senior iOS designer would do it before rebuilding
the interface from the ground up. Reviewed HEAD: `d1c99d2` (build after 395), which carries every fix
from the engineering audit of the same morning (`REPOSITORY_AUDIT_2026-09-24.md`). Five flow critics
each took one surface and read it in full: first run and auth; camera, capture and the sort deck;
feed, comments, activity and pages; rolls, reveal, Live Activity and widget; Darkroom, chapters,
profile, invites and settings. A sixth reviewer distilled every product decision, copy rule,
campaign result and prior design brief in `docs/`. The primary reviewer cross-checked the findings,
read the nightly numbers and the two product memos, and researched the platform and the category.

The output is two documents. This one is the audit: what the app is today, what the numbers say
about it, where a stranger gets lost, and the thesis for v2. The second is the prompt for Claude
Design, `docs/prompts/CLAUDE_DESIGN_V2_FULL_REDESIGN_2026-09-24.md`, the third attempt at a v2
brief, superseding the September 19 and 20 prompts.

**Evidence limits.** No device or simulator was used; every observation is from source and the
recorded data. Live database access was not available in this environment (no analyst token), so
the numbers below are the nightly `NUMBERS.md` lines through September 23, the recorded runs of the
metrics functions in `METRICS.md`, and the product memos of September 14 and 21. Three requested
inputs do not exist in a form the app records:

- **Countries.** The app records no country, region or locale. The only geography FLIM has is the
  website's country breakdown from the host's edge (`web/admin.html:907`), which counts visitors to
  the landing page, not users of the app. What is known about where people are comes from time
  zones: one active account (113 shots, invited by the owner in August) is silent across every
  night at UTC+8, which is why Chapters now take the phone's time zone; the three BALI26 redeemers
  never shot; the promo cards were Italy, Bali and Dubai. Treat FLIM as an Eastern-time app with a
  handful of people abroad, and design the day boundary, the develop clock and the weekly rhythm to
  read correctly in any zone.
- **Per-screen usage.** Launches are not instrumented per day and screens are not instrumented at
  all. "Opened" means shot, posted or reacted. The activation events (`METRICS.md` §1) each began
  logging on a different day and cannot be compared raw.
- **The usability study.** `USABILITY_STUDY.md` was written on September 13 for the owner to run
  before v2. There is no record that it ran. Several decisions below are marked "the study
  decides", as the September 20 prompt already does.

| Shooters per day | 6 to 18 | Two of every three opens end with no photograph. |

Cohorts and funnels, from the recorded runs:

- **Retention.** The September 7 cohort falls from 58% to 26% by week two; the August 10 cohort holds
  near half. The September cohort (20 accounts since September 1): 15 came back another day, 12
  followed someone beyond their inviter, 8 reacted or commented, 7 posted and all 7 were answered.
  Roll side: 2 joined, 1 contributed, 1 watched a reveal.
- **Time to first shot.** Median 130 minutes after account creation (38 accounts, read September 8).
  12 of those 38 never took one. The button said "Take your first shot" and the shot happened two
  hours later, if at all. The one-screen onboarding that replaced the three cards shipped after
  that read; no after measurement exists.
- **Responses.** Every post this month was answered, median 37 to 42 minutes, most within the hour.
  Reaction pushes bring 80 to 100% same-day opens. The response loop works.
- **Thin feeds.** 14 of 50 weekly openers follow fewer than three people, and posts are
  followers-only, so their feed is one or two people's days.
- **Notifications.** 12 active people were never asked; 11 of those 12 are camera-only and never
  touched a roll.
- **Campaigns.** FLIMGO: 13 arrivals, 10 followed nobody but the owner and never shot. BALI26: 3
  redeemers, none shot. SEPT10: zero uses. The invites-left push to 28 people produced one
  invitation. The one thing that moved a number was the thank-you push on September 12, the best
  day on record (71 follows, 5 invites), which fell to nothing the next day.

The shape is unambiguous. FLIM has a working everyday loop for the people who have friends inside
it: they open, they react within the hour, they are answered. It has no working way to make new
friendships inside it, no working occasion mechanic (rolls), no weekly rhythm, and it loses more
than half of a new cohort in two weeks. The camera is used by a third of openers on a given day. The
product's marquee moment, the reveal, has effectively never been seen this month.

## The diagnosis, in five statements

1. **The roll is a premise problem, not a friction problem.** Create is one tap from the tab and
   one field with a prefilled name. It works. Nobody uses it because a roll needs a group, and the
   app never establishes one: the social graph runs through the owner. "Start another with this
   group" has been live since 1.5.3 and has never fired. Until the app can say who a roll is with,
   the create button is a well-designed door to an empty room. The Rolls tab is a quarter of the
   tab bar spent on a feature with zero events for eighteen days.

2. **The app explains instead of showing.** The develop model is taught once, in prose, on a coach
   card, and never again. A personal shot "develops" in zero seconds (`personalDevelopDelay = 0`);
   a roll shot develops in twelve hours; nothing on the camera says either number. Keep versus Post
   is taught for six sorts then retired to a mid-gesture label. The 04:00 day boundary is in code
   comments only. Chapters show only finished months, so a new person looking for "my month" finds
   nothing and no line explaining why. A stranger cannot infer the model from the screens; the
   screens assume the model.

3. **Newcomers arrive alone.** Ten of the last 22 accounts came through a campaign code with nobody
   to follow. The inviter auto-follow and the Find-friends landing are the right fixes and are
   live, but a person whose feed is one friend's Tuesday has no second thing to do. The everyday
   loop needs at least three people in it to turn.

4. **Nothing creates tomorrow.** By deliberate rule there are no streaks, prompts, counts or ranks.
   Right. But the app also has no rhythm of its own: no weekly moment, no occasion, no reason the
   product itself supplies to come back on a day when no friend posted. Retro has the weekly
   journal; BeReal has the daily ping; Lapse has the roll that develops. FLIM has the reveal, which
   nobody reaches. The weekly Spotlight (the Contact Sheet of the September 19 brief) is exactly
   the missing rhythm, and it is the one feature that can give a person with two friends a reason
   to open the app on Monday.

5. **Failures are silent where they matter.** The engineering audits have closed most of the data
   bugs; what remains is a design pattern. A failed reaction has no acknowledgement anywhere. The
   comments sheet's failed send is a haptic with no text. A failed unblock drops the row and says
   nothing. Auth errors fall through to raw system strings. Share is absent rather than refused, so
   "not yours" reads as "no share". v2 needs a rule: every action that can fail shows where it
   failed, in the app's words, with the way back.

## What works, and must survive the redesign

- The film look. One fitted look, byte-identical since 1.5.0, LUT fitted on real pairs, grain
  midtone-peaked and monochrome at 0.06, warm halation, flash falloff put back at 1.0, adaptive
  exposure before the LUT. The owner has tried and rejected variations on device. The single-look
  constraint is also correct UX: it removes a category of decision from a first run that already
  asks a stranger to learn rolls, develop and Keep versus Post.
- The capture chip's five honest states ("Saved on this phone", "Uploading", "Queued on this
  phone", "Uploaded", "Not saved yet") and the camera's new interruption states.
- The sort deck vocabulary: Keep private, Post to page, Delete, with the audience sentence.
- One-tap reactions on visible chips. Reactions are 97% of responses; the first v2 put a tray in
  front of them and the owner felt it.
- The feed's per-author-per-day unit, the unseen ledger that jumps to the first unread frame, and
  the caught-up seam. Nothing ranked, nothing expires.
- Undo-first confirmations with the undo capsule and consequence sheets; hold-to-confirm only for
  account deletion.
- The reveal's pacing (viewer-paced, each frame develops in place once, watched only on
  completion, replays until then), the Live Activity's ring, the lock-screen shutter.
- Tab signals as dots, never numbers. Badges discovered, never pushed. The signup number as a frame
  edge number.
- The copy rules: Post versus Share, page not feed, no exclamation marks, no em dashes, never
  "friends" where the truth is "people who follow you".

## Surface by surface

### First run and auth

Six taps past typing an email to a first frame, one wait for the emailed code. Good. What is
wrong is around it, not in it:

- Every network failure in the chain (send code, verify, resend, save username) surfaces the
  system's own error string. A stranger's first error is engineer text.
- The invite code field and the roll code field are visually identical six-character fields with
  the same placeholder, meaning different things. A person holding a roll code tries it at sign-in.
- The notification primer promises "the moment a roll you're in develops" before the word roll has
  been met.
- The OTP wait has no bound stated; silence before "check your spam folder" reads as failure.
- "PICK YOUR COLOR" reads as decoration; nothing says it recolours the app.
- Founding 100 running out has no sign-in-time state; the badge's own copy says "that window's
  closed" only to people who missed it.

### Camera and capture

The camera is the best screen in the app: one look, flash, timer, roll pill, zoom pills, a
breathing shutter, honest states. What a stranger cannot learn from it:

- When a roll develops. The only clock is the last hour's "18m left". A person shooting into a
  roll on day two has no way to learn the reveal time without leaving the camera.
- That a personal shot has no wait at all. "Shoot now. See it later." sits beside "straight
  away" and the two cancel out.
- That double-tap flips the camera (never taught) and that the volume rocker shoots (taught by an
  invisible tip).
- Whether the front flash is a screen brighten (it is).
- Missing states: storage full, queue full; no camera-facing sign that a burst was detected or that
  a frame was judged Missed before the sort deck.

### Darkroom and the sort deck

The Darkroom's zoom ladder (month, year, all-time), the nights as film racks, the develop wells and
the first-frame state are strong. The problems are about legibility of the model:

- Kept and posted are interleaved with a 14 by 2 point accent dash as the only mark. This is the
  single most load-bearing distinction a new person must learn and it is nearly invisible.
- Unsorted shots are not in the rack; they exist only behind the "N shots to sort" banner. A person
  who dismisses the banner has lost their unsorted shots until they find it again.
- The Keep/Post swipe hint retires after six sorts; from the seventh, the only signal that right
  is public is a drag label after the gesture is past its threshold.
- The sort deck's cards look identical whether the frame is sharp, blurred, black, or one of a
  burst; the pipeline knows (quality, phash, isMiss, burst group) and the card does not say.
- Share is absent for frames you do not own, with no line saying why.

### Feed, comments, activity, pages

The feed card, the unit, the ledger, the seam, the block-with-undo and the follow-gated page are
right. The gaps:

- Comment is the invisible response. 3% of responses. The entry is a faint "Add a comment" line.
- The caught-up block reads the same whether you finished today's feed or nobody you follow has
  ever posted.
- The double-tap heart never removes; a second double-tap does nothing and no copy says so.
- Reaction failure: no acknowledgement anywhere. Comment-sheet send failure: a haptic, no text.
- A post deleted mid-session vanishes from the feed with no "isn't here anymore" state, unlike the
  push and Activity paths which have one.
- The 04:00 boundary is invisible in copy; a 2am shot files under yesterday with no explanation.
- The audience of a post is said at the sort deck (good) but nowhere on the card.
- `navigationDestination` inside lazy containers at four sites; SwiftUI warns today and will break
  later. A v2 navigation model should hoist destinations to the stack.

### Rolls and the reveal

The most mature surface in the app: every state the critics looked for has a designed screen
(one member, zero frames, all dead frames, member left, creator deleted, joined before sign-in,
expired link, second device). The reveal is good. The problem is the premise (statement 1) and
three legibility gaps:

- "Nobody sees a frame until then, you included" is a one-time first-visit line; afterwards a
  developing tile could read as an upload that failed.
- Nothing tells a first-timer that leaving a reveal at frame 3 of 40 is safe (it replays) versus
  finishing it (it is then seen).
- "Develops together" can be read as "FLIM assembles something"; it is a visibility gate, never
  said so.

### Profile, chapters, identity, invites, settings

- The page is a stack of five ideas in build order (cover, identity, stats, chapters, grid), with
  what people come for at the bottom. The September 8 profile brief already says this.
- Chapters show finished months only; the current month has no slot and no line.
- The chapter opening card shows immediately with whatever the shelf resolved; "still computing" is
  invisible on the card.
- 22 badges, 19 earned automatically. Quiet by design, but the locked catalogue reads as a
  checklist, close to the line the house rule draws.
- The invite earn-back (you get an invite back when your invitee takes a first photo) is the one
  growth mechanic and it is small print under the button.
- The branded export carries the wordmark and a date and nothing else: no handle, no way in. That
  is a chosen austerity and a real growth cost; v2 should decide it rather than inherit it.
- Delete account now says what will happen and cannot lie (fixed this morning).

## The category and the platform, September 2026

**The category.** Lapse (4.8 stars, 118K ratings) owns "friends not followers" and the roll that
develops; its recent update drew complaints for showing friends in a story-style row, which its
users read as a betrayal of the journal feel. Retro is a weekly friends-only photo journal with
monthly recaps, hidden friend lists, private likes, postcards, and in 2026 group albums and group
messaging; its whole rhythm is weekly. Dispo's users report ads made it unusable. BeReal is the
daily ping; Locket is the widget on a friend's home screen; Roll launched this year as another
disposable camera. The lesson for FLIM: the austere, no-ads, no-ranks position is shared by the
best of these, so it is table stakes, not a differentiator. What differentiates is the look (fitted,
not a filter), the reveal (when it happens), the Darkroom (private first), and, if v2 builds it,
the weekly sheet with a human editor. Nobody in the category has an editor.

**The platform.** iOS 27 shipped this month with Liquid Glass reworked: lower default transparency,
a user slider from clear to tinted, sharper icons, and the `UIDesignRequiresCompatibility` opt-out
ignored once an app builds with Xcode 27, so every app is glass at the navigation layer whether it
chose to be or not. The Human Interface Guidelines are explicit that glass belongs to the
navigation layer floating above content and never to the content layer, and that glass over a flat
background reads as a tinted rectangle. The system tab bar floats and minimises on scroll, changing
safe-area insets; custom tab bars get no glass. iOS 27's Camera app made its controls customisable
and put a mode-settings button beside the shutter; Photos added AI editing. For FLIM this means:
glass for the tab bar, toolbars, the capsule buttons and the sheets' chrome, opaque near-black for
every surface a photograph sits on, never glass over a photograph, never a glass card in a list,
and the 1.5.4 `glassCard` used on content surfaces should go. FLIM's own dark, content-first design
is where the guidelines already point.

## The thesis for v2

FLIM v2 is not a new look and not a feature list. It is the same product made legible and given a
rhythm. Every screen shows the model instead of explaining it (a roll shows its clock, a kept frame
looks kept, a post says who sees it). Newcomers arrive into a feed with people in it. The week has a
shape: post through the week, put one frame on the sheet, Monday the editor circles a few. Rolls
stop being a tab nobody visits and become an occasion the app notices and offers. Every screen
ships in every state it can be in, including the ones where the network is gone, the data is
partial, or a race just happened. And the whole thing must sit beside Apple Photos, Journal and
Instagram on the same phone and belong there, unmistakably FLIM: near-black, one warm accent, SF
Pro, the photograph as the only colour, film as a material (rebate, perforation, grease pencil,
seven-segment date) and never as a costume.

The ten principles the prompt is built on:

1. The photograph is the product. 3:4, full width minus margin, never inset for chrome.
2. Show the model, never explain it. A number where there is a clock, a mark where there is a
   state, a sentence where there is an audience.
3. One tap to answer. Reactions stay one tap; Comment becomes a real control.
4. Every screen ships in nine states (see the prompt, section 8), and the offline state loses
   nothing and says so.
5. Failures land in place, in the app's words, with the way back. Nothing fails silently.
6. Rhythm without pressure. A weekly sheet with an editor; no streaks, counts, ranks or prompts.
7. Rolls are an occasion the app offers, not a tab the person must remember.
8. Glass on the navigation layer only. Opaque ground under every photograph.
9. Accessible by construction: 44pt, AA on the composited surface, AX3, VoiceOver, Reduce Motion.
10. Copy is the design. Short, warm, specific, FLIM's words, no exclamation marks, no em dashes.

## Decisions for the owner before the prompt runs

Each is a one-line answer; the prompt carries them as questions if unanswered.

1. **The weekly feature's name.** You said "Spotlight" today. Your September 19 and 20 briefs say
   it must not be called Spotlight, Featured, Highlights, Best of or Picks, and name it the Contact
   Sheet, verb circled, because it is a film object no other app has. The prompt uses Contact Sheet
   and circled as the working names and flags the decision; one find-and-replace changes it.
   Recommendation: keep Contact Sheet. It is the only name in the category that is not already
   owned by someone else, and the grease-pencil circle is a better mark than a badge.
2. **Rolls as a tab.** Zero rolls in eighteen days argues for folding rolls into Camera (the roll
   pill) and Feed (a developed roll is a day unit with several authors), keeping the reveal, the
   Live Activity and the widget. The prompt asks for both architectures drawn.
3. **Launch surface and tab order.** Still "the study decides". If the study will not run, say so
   and let the design decide; the prompt cannot draw two apps.
4. **Comments.** A real control beside the chips, at the cost of some card height, or leave them
   at 3%.
5. **Founding 100.** It ends in about six weeks. What replaces the scarcity, if anything.
6. **The export.** Whether a shared photograph should ever carry a way into FLIM.
7. **The admin surface for the sheet.** The web admin panel (a new queue card, one circle action)
   or an in-app owner mode. The prompt designs the web queue and asks.
