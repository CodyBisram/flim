## Flim/Services/InstantFilmProcessor.swift:631-632 (severity: medium)

The near-black safety floor in `flashFalloff` does not do what its own comment says it does.

Failure scenario: the comment above the line states that below the 0.02 threshold "the peak is treated as 1" so an already-black frame is "left essentially untouched instead of amplifying its brightest speck into a fake subject." The code instead computes `let peak = max(0.02, areaMaximum(...))` and then `let gain = 1 / peak`. For a flash-fired capture (EXIF flash bit set) whose subject is out of the flash's reach, for example a flash photo of something distant or dark at night, the coarse illumination map's peak luma can fall below 0.02. In that case `peak` is floored to 0.02, not treated as 1, giving `gain = 50` rather than `gain = 1`. That gain of 50 propagates through the clamp, gamma, and lift-into-`[flashFalloffFloor, 1]` stages and crushes the frame's multiplier map toward the low end instead of leaving it near 1, so the already near-black frame gets darkened further by roughly another 70 to 80 percent rather than being left essentially untouched as documented. This is a real, narrow class of photos (flash-fired but effectively unlit frames from a disposable-camera-style flash with no reachable subject) and is untested: `FlashFalloffTests.swift` has no fixture with a sub-0.02 map peak.
