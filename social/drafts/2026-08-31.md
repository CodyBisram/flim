# FLIM social drafts, week of Aug 31

Covers the reveal losing its timer, the export dropping its paper border for a
burned-in date, every grid switching to the whole uncropped negative, and early
flash-falloff work in the film simulation. All of it is on the unreleased train,
so every post frames it as building or testing.

## X

### 1. No more clock
> FLIM's reveal used to run on a clock. Frames advanced whether you were ready or not. We took the timer out. A developed roll opens at your own pace now, one frame at a time, and a strip along the bottom holds the whole roll so you can jump back to any shot.

Why now: this is the change a person feels first, and it landed across the reveal this cycle.

### 2. No second crop
> FLIM crops every capture to 3:4 once, on the camera. Some grids were cropping it again to force a square tile, so the same photo looked different depending on where you saw it. We pulled that second crop out. What you framed is what you see, everywhere in the app.

Why now: the profile grid, roll grid, and Darkroom covers all moved to the same rule this week.

### 3. A date back
> We tried giving exported photos a paper border, like a print. It was the wrong object. FLIM is a roll, not a single Polaroid. So the paper is gone, and the date is burned into the corner instead, the way a real camera's date back works. The photo stays the photo.

Why now: the export redesign landed this cycle and it is the most physical, camera-like detail in the pile.

### 4. Teaching the flash to fall off
> A disposable camera's flash lights what's close and drops everything behind it toward black. We're building that into FLIM's film simulation now, gated so it only touches photos actually shot with flash. Testing it in TestFlight before it goes further.

Why now: honest build-in-public material on work that's real but not yet verified end to end.

Why now: pairs the Darkroom's larger frame size with the full-frame grid work into one visual.

### 2. Date back
Asset: one photograph shot through FLIM (own account, seeded or personal
content), exported full-bleed with the new burned-in date mark visible in the
corner, warm evening light.
> FLIM burns the date into the corner now. No paper, no border. Just the photo and when it was taken.

Why now: the strongest new visual signature from this cycle, and easy to show in a single export.

### 3. The reveal without the clock
Asset: short screen-capture clip (under 10s), paging through a developed roll
frame by frame at a deliberately unhurried pace, seeded demo content, ending on
the film-strip scrubber at the bottom.
> Nothing moves on without you. Page through a developed roll at your own speed.

Why now: motion shows the removed timer better than any still can.
